Free · Open source · Works off-grid

When the internet goes dark, stay in reach.

One app for messages, people and sites. When the internet is up it delivers over the internet; when it is not, over Beacon's own network; when both work, over both. No account and no server, and with no internet at all a single hotspot is enough.

Planned capability

No tower, no router, no server

Beacon has no central server. Everything lives on your own devices, so it keeps working with no infrastructure at all.

01

A hotspot is a network

Any hotspot or router is enough, even with no internet behind it. No mobile data needed.

02

No server anywhere

No account, no cloud, no phone number. Messages and sites stay on your devices.

03

Carry it on USB

Take messages and sites from one place to another on a flash drive.

04

The app passes hand to hand

Beacon shares its own installer over local Wi-Fi, so new people can join with no internet.

Off-grid range is Wi-Fi range: a room, a floor, a building. Radio bridges (LoRa, Bluetooth) come later.

How far can you reach?

Beacon works out which paths are open. It sends over the internet when it can, over its own network when it cannot, and over both when both are up.

Beacon never knocks on strangers' addresses. It only connects to people and lighthouses you were introduced to.

How Beacon is designed to work

Questions

How does it work without the internet?

When the internet is up, messages go over it. When it is not, Beacon uses whatever is left: your Wi-Fi or LAN, your provider's own network, your country's domestic routes. If all of that is down, it works on the same Wi-Fi only.

Do phones need an app?

No. Scan a nearby computer's QR code and it opens in the browser. An Android app comes later.

Can anyone read my messages?

No, they are end-to-end encrypted. When a message travels over the internet, the relays in between see only encrypted text. This version has no forward secrecy yet: if your key is stolen, old messages can be read. If you join from a phone, your messages are kept on that computer and the person who runs it can read them.

Does it cost anything?

No. It is free and open source under the MIT license.

Why does Windows show a warning?

New apps can trigger SmartScreen. Verify the publisher and checksum before running a build. Signing does not guarantee a warning-free download; do not turn Windows protection off.

Is it useful while the internet works?

Yes. It is an everyday app: it uses the internet when it is up, and when the internet drops you change nothing. It carries on over Beacon's network by itself.
